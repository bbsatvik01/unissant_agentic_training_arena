### (a) Keeping role-play agents in character and grounded to a fact packet

The Black Hat project's E6 (role-play/simulation/wargaming survey) is the closest analog to SUB-1..SUB-8 (simulated subjects playing a truthful/deceptive role from a private profile). Key transferable findings:

- **Persona labels don't change behavior; facts do.** Lamparth et al. (AAAI/ACM AIES 2024, arXiv 2403.03407) found LLM teams in wargames "ignore persona traits" — "aggressive" vs "pacifist" produced no significant behavioral difference. The actionable design rule: our subject profiles (SCN-8) must be dense, structured fact packets (Background / relevant actions / what the subject knows / permitted disclosure / uncertainty limits / objective), never adjective-only personas. Confidence: high (peer-reviewed, directly on point).
- **Structured schema beats raw transcript volume.** Ye/Deng/Candogan's BDE schema (Background/Decision-procedure/Evaluation) added +1.9pp over raw dumps (arXiv 2608.20344). For our SCN-8 private subject packets, organizing facts under a fixed schema (role, known facts, uncertainty limits, permitted behaviors, objective) rather than free prose should improve consistency. Confidence: medium (small effect, preprint).
- **Persona drift over long dialogues is real and only partially fixable.** Leins et al. (arXiv 2609.24532, 2026-09-21) measured 28-turn conversations: re-injecting the persona each turn cut drift 35-38%, reflective reminders 22-27%, monitor-generated reminders 87% — but "none eliminated drift." Direct implication for KPI-3 (≥95% subject fidelity) and BKO-8 (up to 12-exchange interviews): re-inject the subject's permitted-behavior list and uncertainty limits at every turn, not just at session start; budget for periodic fidelity re-grounding. Confidence: high (recent, specific measurement).
- **Persona collapse: the most "faithful" models are the most stereotyped.** Xiao et al., "The Chameleon's Limit" (arXiv 2604.24698, 2026-04-27): across 10 LLMs, the models achieving highest per-persona fidelity also produced the most stereotyped populations. For SCN-11 (matched truthful/deceptive case families with style varied independently of role — PRB-4's anti-shortcut requirement), this is a direct risk: if many subject runs look identical in style regardless of assigned role, the arena cannot test whether assistants use style as a false shortcut (which PRB-4 explicitly forbids). Mitigation: measure population diversity of subject outputs and treat near-identical style across different style assignments as a fidelity defect to catch before scoring. Confidence: medium (one preprint, not peer-reviewed, but directly relevant to a named requirement).
- **Suppress parametric knowledge beyond the fact packet.** RoleFact-style instruction ("if it is not in your profile, you do not know it; mark as uncertain") gave +18% factual precision and -44% temporal hallucination on adversarial questions in a different domain (TimeChara/RoleFact, [>12mo], cited in E6). Direct fit to SUB-2 ("truthful roles answer from known facts and may be uncertain") and SUB-6/SUB-7 (deceptive roles must not fabricate facts contradicting canonical truth — "any statement that contradicts a canonical fact is a fidelity failure" per REQUIREMENTS.md working interpretation #1). Confidence: medium (RoleFact evidence is >12mo old but the design pattern is architecture-agnostic).
- **Independent/first-pass generation beats multi-round "discussion."** Across seven benchmarks, majority voting (not genuine deliberation) explains most "debate" gains (Choi/Zhu/Li, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2508.17536); debate can reduce accuracy through conformity (Wynn et al. 2025; Hao et al. 2026: 37% of answers flip under self-reflection alone, 57-77% of flips go correct-to-wrong). Implication for AST/SUB interaction (BKO-7's interactive track): the simulated subject should answer independently each turn from its fixed packet, not "negotiate" with any other agent or revise its story to stay consistent with a prior turn's drift — consistency should come from re-grounding against canonical facts (SCN-6/SCN-10: "the underlying event never changes to accommodate a subject's answer"), not from social consensus-seeking. Confidence: high (multiple independent 2025-2026 sources agree).
- **Score fidelity and role-success separately** (already in SUB-8/EVL-11). E6's framing — "a deceptive subject succeeds only if the violation stays undetected within budget while obeying the scenario" vs. "invalid profile behavior is a fidelity failure, not successful evasion" — is exactly SUB-8/EVL-11's design and the research corroborates treating these as two independent axes, not one blended score.

### (b) Judge and rubric design and calibration

N10 (exemplars/quality rubric for black-hat briefs) is the TechnoMile analog of our AST/EVL judge problem (a coach that must flag gaps/contradictions, give findings with confidence, and be scored itself by a trainer/judge).

- **Binary checklists beat 1-5 Likert scales for LLM-judge reliability.** CheckEval (arXiv 2403.18771) improved cross-model judge agreement by 0.45 and reduced score variance by decomposing criteria into binary pass/fail sub-checks; TICK (arXiv 2410.03608) confirms. A practitioner source (Hamel Husain) independently argues "what makes something a 3 vs 4? Nobody knows" for 1-5 scales. Direct implication for EVL-7 (investigator rubric: five 0-4 dimensions) and AST-9/AST-10 (assistant findings graded under a false-positive constraint): implement the 0-4 anchors as the HUMAN-facing scale, but have the LLM judge answer a set of binary sub-checks per dimension and derive the 0-4 score deterministically from the pass fraction (anchor = 1 + round(4 × pass_fraction)), exactly as N10's rubric (section 3) does for its own 10-criterion weighted rubric. Confidence: high (two independent peer-reviewed/arXiv sources plus a converging practitioner source).
- **Judge bias mitigations**: use a judge model from a different family than the generator where possible (reduces self-enhancement bias, Zheng et al. 2023 "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge," arXiv 2306.05685), judge per-section rather than the whole artifact (limits verbosity bias), and calibrate against 20-40 human-labeled examples with measured agreement (target κ ≥ 0.6, N10's own proposal, not independently sourced). Gemini-only constraint (owner lock) means "different family" judges are not available for us; mitigate instead with structured decomposition and independent sampling rather than cross-vendor diversity.
- **Hard gates before soft scores.** N10's rubric makes factual accuracy/citation fidelity a *gate*: if it scores ≤2/5, the whole brief is capped at 40/100 regardless of other dimensions. This maps directly onto our BKO-10/EVL-10 structure: false-positive rate ≤5% and isolation/leakage pass are gates before ranking by recall — N10's pattern of "gate, then score" is corroborating prior art for exactly this architecture, just at the artifact-quality level rather than the leaderboard level. Confidence: high (direct structural analog, already a locked requirement in our own REQUIREMENTS.md).
- **Atomic-claim and citation-level scoring.** FActScore (atomic-fact support rate, Min et al. arXiv 2305.14251) and ALCE (citation recall/precision, Gao et al. arXiv 2305.14627) are the standard measurement units for "does this specific cited span actually support this specific claim" — directly reusable for AST-7's requirement that every flag cite an exact transcript turn and name a missing/conflicting fact: implement a deterministic validator that checks the quoted span exists verbatim in the cited turn, not just that a turn number was cited. Confidence: high (established NLP evaluation methods).
- **Calibrated-uncertainty vocabulary (ICD 203).** Both E6 and N10 independently converge on ICD 203 (signed 2015, amended 2022) as the external anchor for "defensible" confidence language: a fixed 7-term likelihood ladder (almost no chance 01-05% ... almost certain 95-99%), a rule that confidence-level and likelihood must never appear in the same sentence, and a requirement to label facts vs. assumptions vs. judgments explicitly and name "indicators that would change the judgment." This maps very directly onto AST-3 ("finding with confidence") and EVL-12's own citation of NIST AI RMF; ICD 203 is a complementary, more operational vocabulary the arena could adopt for how a coaching assistant phrases its running finding. Confidence: high (primary government standard, both routes independently landed on it).
- **Hallucination-prone claim typing with deterministic verifiers.** N10 built a 14-type taxonomy of claim types an LLM reliably gets wrong in the GovCon domain (contract values with 3 valid definitions, stale/reversed winners after protests, non-public ratings, redacted values, date arithmetic errors, etc.), each paired with an automated, deterministic check against an authoritative source. The *pattern* (not the specific GovCon types) transfers: any claim type with more than one valid-looking numeric representation, or with a "freshness" dimension (a status that can flip), is a hallucination vector and needs a typed field (not free prose) plus a deterministic re-check. For our arena, analogous risk classes likely include: timeline/evidence-release state (SCN-10's "every change is versioned and timestamped" argues for the same discipline), and scoring-key fields (which must stay typed and hidden per SCN-5/SCN-9). Confidence: medium (pattern transfer from an adjacent but different domain).

Source: TechnoMile .../docs/research/external/N10-exemplars-and-quality-rubric.md (sections 2.5, 3, 5).

### (c) Prompt-injection and data-isolation defenses between agents

N5 is the most load-bearing finding set for SUB-7 ("the subject must not rewrite the underlying event or attack the actual platform... prompt-injection attempts make the session invalid") and for the general multi-agent architecture (SOL-2/SOL-3/SOL-4's separation of assistant, subject, and scenario-engine roles).

- **No classifier or prompt technique reliably stops indirect prompt injection; treat detectors as telemetry, not a boundary.** The strongest 2025 evidence (14 authors across OpenAI/Anthropic/Google DeepMind, "The Attacker Moves Second," arXiv 2510.09023) shows adaptive attacks beat 12 published defenses at >90% success, including Google's own Model Armor. Google's own Gemini defense paper (arXiv 2505.14534) reaches the same conclusion: "more capable models aren't necessarily more secure... defenses in depth at each layer" are required, and in-context defenses (spotlighting, paraphrasing, warnings) show "minimal effectiveness" under adaptive attack. Confidence: high (primary, cross-lab, very recent).
- **Architecture, not classifiers, is the boundary: Rule of Two / lethal trifecta.** Meta's "Agents Rule of Two" and Simon Willison's "lethal trifecta" (private data + untrusted content + external communication = exfiltration risk) give a concrete separation principle. Direct translation to our arena: the deceptive-subject agent's output is, by construction, content an adversarial party (the simulated subject, possibly jailbroken per threat T15 in N5) controls — it must be treated as **untrusted input** to the coaching assistant, exactly like a hostile webpage is untrusted input to a research worker in N5's threat model. The coaching assistant (AST) should never have an outbound/state-changing channel that the subject's text can steer (no tool calls driven by subject-authored text), and the scenario engine's locked facts/scoring key (SCN-9, hidden from both assistants and investigators) must live in a context the subject-playing agent's own generation never has write access to — i.e., the subject agent should not be the keeper of ground truth; a separate, privileged component should be. This is the same "dual LLM" / "privileged planner vs. quarantined reader" pattern N5 recommends (C1: privilege-separated topology) adapted to our roles: Scenario Engine (privileged, holds SCN-9 ground truth) → Subject Agent (plays role, no access to the scoring key, no outbound tool) → transcript (the only channel to the Assistant) → Assistant Agent (treats transcript text as data to analyze, never as instructions to execute). Confidence: high for the general architecture pattern (N5's C1), medium for the specific role-mapping (my inference, not independently validated in either corpus).
- **A jailbroken "deceptive subject" producing defamatory/unsupported claims is an explicitly named threat (N5's T15).** N5 flags exactly this: "role-play agent (competitor persona) is jailbroken into producing defamatory or unsupported claims." For us this maps onto SUB-6/SUB-7's permitted-behavior boundary — the deceptive subject's "permitted disclosure policy" and "high-level evasive behaviors such as omission or deflection" needs the same instruction-suppression discipline N5 recommends for research workers (C3: content delimiting/spotlighting as hygiene, not a boundary; C2: structured I/O, no free-form tool arguments). Because our subjects do not have fetch/outbound tools (unlike N5's research workers), the SSRF/exfiltration-channel findings (C5, C6, F12-F17 — metadata-server, DNS rebinding, etc.) are LOWER priority for the subject/assistant pair specifically, but remain directly relevant to any other part of the arena that does fetch external content (e.g., if scenario authoring or case generation pulls real-world reference material). Confidence: high that the threat is real and named; medium on exact applicability since our subject agents are closed-world (no internet access), which removes N5's SSRF-specific channel but not the "untrusted generated text as input to the next agent" channel (T1/T2 analogs).
- **Citations as both a win condition and a security control** (N5's F4/C10). N5's claim-ledger pattern — every claim carries a source_id plus a verbatim quote validated as a substring of the stored snapshot, with the brief/UI rendering links only from the ledger, never from LLM text — maps directly onto AST-7's requirement that "a flag must cite an exact transcript turn and name the relevant missing fact or conflicting statement... a flag without a valid turn citation counts as unsupported (EVL-5)." The deterministic validator design (does the cited turn actually contain the claimed conflict/gap, checked in code, not just asserted by the LLM) is directly portable. Confidence: high (same pattern validated by two independent routes — N5 and N10 — converging on the same mechanism from different angles).
- **Typed outputs, not free-form tool arguments, limit what an injected or jailbroken agent can do (N5's C2).** AST-8's closed-category enum (non-responsive / contradiction / explicit uncertainty / possible evasion) already follows this pattern; N5 corroborates it as a security control, not just a UX nicety — closed enums and Pydantic-validated structured output bound what a manipulated upstream agent can inject downstream.
- **Supply-chain and secrets hygiene items that transfer directly regardless of domain:** uv lockfile + 7-day dependency cooldown with a security fast-lane, GitHub Actions pinned by SHA, never deploy dev UIs (ADK's `adk web` carries two 2026 CVEs, one CVSS 10), Phoenix auth is OFF by default self-hosted (`PHOENIX_ENABLE_AUTH=true` required) — directly relevant since our BRIEF also locks "Observability: Arize Phoenix, maximum coverage," and N5 explicitly flags Phoenix traces (A8) as a secondary data store that "often left unauthenticated" and containing uploads/private content. Confidence: high (primary CVE/advisory sources).

Source: TechnoMile .../docs/research/external/N5-agent-security-prompt-injection-ssrf.md (lines 1-30 "bottom line," F1-F6, F11, T1-T15 threat register, C1-C10).

### (d) UI patterns for agents visibly working, citations, live progress, motion

E13 (UI/UX state of the art) and N9 (forkable app shells) are directly reusable for BRIEF's "agent avatars, companion pets, talking agents, agents visibly doing work, virtual interrogation room" requirement and SOL-1's "text transcript is the scored artifact; any voice/avatar layer is presentation only."

- **Converged pattern across 6+ leading agentic products** (OpenAI Deep Research, Gemini Deep Research, Perplexity, Manus, AlphaSense, Hebbia): editable plan before execution → live progress in a secondary pane as *named steps*, not raw tokens → mid-run steering → split pane (process left / artifact right) → resumable long runs → export to document, not chat text. This is a strong template for a "virtual interrogation room" screen: plan/scenario-setup pane, live transcript + coaching pane, and an evidence/finding side panel. Confidence: high (6+ independent products, 2025-2026, cross-checked against GitHub/npm metadata).
- **Documented anti-patterns, directly relevant to "agents visibly doing work":** chat-by-default, hiding the process entirely, *and* "showing the full trace by default" (Agent UX Patterns Catalog Vol. 13, May 2026) — "show everything live" is explicitly called out as a demo trap; progressive disclosure (summary always visible, detail on demand) is the consensus resolution. Since our product's win condition is coaching **within 3 seconds** (KPI-4) and the coach must evaluate "the officer's latest question in context" (AST-6) turn by turn, the UI needs to show the assistant visibly working per-turn (latency budget is tight) without defaulting to a full chain-of-thought dump that would slow comprehension during a live interview. Confidence: high (same conclusion from two independent routes, E13 section 2.2 and 6.1, and TechnoMile's own LESSONS entries on their War Room/agent-office build).
- **Confidence display: categorical, not raw percentages.** Hebbia shows "High/Medium/Low conviction," not numeric percentages; Xiong et al. (ICLR 2024, arXiv 2306.13063) establish that raw verbalized LLM confidence is poorly calibrated. Direct implication for AST-3's "finding with confidence": render confidence categorically (or via the ICD 203 ladder from (b) above), never as a bare unvalidated percentage. Confidence: high for the calibration problem (ICLR, well established); medium for "categorical beats numeric" as a UI choice (one vendor example, contested in E13's own section 6 — Smashing Magazine's Feb 2026 guidance suggests percentages/visual cues are also viable, so this is a genuine open design choice, not settled).
- **Citation UX: ID-mapped citations, not LLM-typed URLs.** Morphic's pattern (`citationMaps[toolCallId][n]` resolving a numbered chip to a concrete tool-result item, versus DeerFlow's weaker pattern of regex-parsing `[citation: Title](url)` markdown the LLM typed) is the stronger, more secure pattern — the citation number must resolve through code to a ledger entry, never be trusted as typed by the model. This is the same principle as N5's C10 and N10's atomic-claim checks (section b/c above), now triangulated from a third, independent (UI-engineering) research route. Confidence: high (source code read directly in three repos).
- **TechnoMile's own build log (LESSONS, 2026-10-03/04) is a live case study of exactly what the owner wants for our BRIEF's "agents visibly doing work / talking agents / pets" language**: they built a MiroFish-inspired (design-only, no AGPL code) "agent office" — a 2.5D floor view with desks per agent, researchers walking to a web kiosk, a verification desk with a stamp animation, a board room for evaluator agents, idle "blink" animation as the one exception to "loops only mark work in progress," all driven strictly off recorded run events (no animation without a corresponding event), with `prefers-reduced-motion` collapsing all of it to static captions. This is a directly reusable UI/UX pattern (not code, since it's a sibling proprietary build, but the *pattern* — event-driven character animation with a documented rule "every movement is one recorded event; nothing moves without one" — is exactly the "companion pets / talking agents" brief requirement already built and verified at the motion/performance level (0 layout shift, p95 16.8ms frame pacing, verified 2026-09-28/2026-10-04)). Confidence: high (first-hand verification entries in the sibling project's own design docs, dated and measured).
- **Observability-as-UI-feature: deep-link every claim to its Phoenix trace span.** E13 found Phoenix ships `/redirects/spans/:span_otel_id` routes, enabling a "how we know this" link from any UI claim straight to the exact span that produced it — a genuine "wow moment" that is also a real accountability mechanism, directly aligned with our BRIEF's "Observability: Arize Phoenix, maximum coverage" constraint and with AST-7's citation requirement. Confidence: high for the route existing (official docs read); medium that it behaves as expected in a deployed instance (not hands-on tested in that route).
- **Stack currency caveat:** E13/N9's library version table (Next 16.3.6, React 19.3, AI Elements 1.9.0, AG-UI 1.0.0 as of 2026-09-17, etc.) is dated 2026-09-25, inside the "flag anything older than 12 months" window, but this entire area moves fast enough that these exact versions should be re-verified at build time rather than assumed current for our project, which starts later.

### (e) The design-system practice the owner used last time

This is directly transferable *process*, not content (their palette/brand is Mistral-derived and explicitly not to be copied verbatim by us, since BRIEF says "Owner will supply a DESIGN.md" for OUR project separately).

- **Method:** the owner supplied a `DESIGN.md` built by extracting a real product's (Mistral AI's) design tokens into a single frontmatter-YAML + prose doc (colors, typography scale, spacing scale, named components as token-composed objects, do's/don'ts, responsive breakpoints, "known gaps"). DECISIONS.md records: "docs/design/DESIGN.md = VoltAgent awesome-design-md mistral.ai DESIGN.md (MIT). Use its design language (tokens, type, layout rhythm); never copy Mistral's logo, wordmark or brand name." I.e., the practice is: license a design-language extraction tool/format (VoltAgent's awesome-design-md, MIT), point it at an admired product, get a structured DESIGN.md, then explicitly remap tokens in a second `tokens.md` document to your own product's semantics (not 1:1 color reuse) with WRITTEN JUSTIFICATION for every deviation (e.g., "fa520f on white is 3.34:1, below AA for a 14px label; #cc3a05 is 5.03:1" — i.e., accessibility-driven token changes, documented inline). Confidence: high (both files read directly, source of truth stated explicitly).
- **A `tokens.md` "mapping table" pattern**: DESIGN.md value → App value → Why, covering action color, surface meaning (their rule: "one meaning per surface" — cream = "a person decides or a source is quoted here"), canvas tint, display type substitution (commercial PP Editorial Old → OFL Newsreader, "same editorial-serif-over-sans contrast, free license"), radius/elevation (kept as-is), and crucially a **stance/semantics layer that has nothing to do with the donor brand**: "black hat = ink band and filled glyph; white hat = outline and outline glyph... never red versus green" and a full state-glyph vocabulary for their evidence-matrix cells (pending/anchored/verified/contested/withheld), each with both a glyph AND a word so it reads in grayscale. This generalizes directly to our arena's need for a similarly disciplined truthful/deceptive-role-neutral visual vocabulary (PRB-4 explicitly forbids style-based shortcuts — a parallel discipline to "never red vs green" for stance). Confidence: high.
- **Accessibility and mode verification baked into the same doc, not deferred**: documented WCAG 2.2 contrast ratios for every named color against the actual canvas tint used (not the donor's), light-first rationale backed by a measurement ("G8 measured 56-57% contrast loss for dark screens under room light"), a presenter mode (`[data-presenter="on"]` at 137.5% text, rem-based reflow) for projector demos, and axe-core automated checks reported as "0 serious or critical violations on every screen at 1440x900" with dated screenshots in `docs/design/screens/`. This whole "measure and record, don't assert" discipline is a transferable process practice independent of the specific tokens. Confidence: high.
- **A companion `motion.md` with the same rigor**: named semantic tokens for duration/easing (not ad hoc), a table mapping every animation utility to its single permitted "meaning" ("one meaning per animation... a new animation needs a new meaning"), a hard rule (transform/opacity only, no layout-triggering properties), explicit reduced-motion collapse behavior, and dated, numeric verification entries (frame pacing p50/p95, layout-shift score, "0 running animations longer than 1ms... on the streaming War Room (25 without it)"). This is a strong template for our own motion needs (SOL "agents visibly doing work," KPI-4's 3-second coaching latency meaning the UI must show *fast* per-turn feedback without jank).
- **Caveat/non-transferable part:** the actual Mistral-derived palette, PP Editorial Old typeface, and "sunset stripe" signature band are explicitly NOT ours to reuse (different donor brand, different product); only the *practice* (extract → remap with written rationale → verify numerically → record dated screenshots) transfers, not the specific tokens.

### (f) De-duplicated lessons applicable to the arena build

Deduplicated from `docs/handoff/LESSONS.md` (34 entries read in full) and cross-referenced against DECISIONS.md. Each keeps its source dated entry for traceability.

1. **Verify the FINAL exported artifact, not just the underlying research.** A hand-built brief scored only 58.8-67% claim-supported in its evidence stage, but the exported one-pager scored only 29% — summarization dropped qualifiers ("will expand to 60+" → "runs 60+") and merged facts across sources. *Directly transferable*: our scorer/EVL pipeline must re-verify the FINAL finding/flag text an assistant outputs, not just trust that its underlying reasoning was grounded — AST-7's "exact transcript turn" citation must be checked against the rendered flag text, not an intermediate draft. Source: LESSONS 2026-09-25 "Product lesson (gold run)."
2. **A self-reported verdict is not the source of truth; re-derive it from the artifact.** A critic agent's structured "yellow, 0 blockers" summary disagreed with its own markdown report (which said RED, 4 blockers). Fix: an independent reader re-derives the verdict from the file, never trusts a self-reported field. Transferable to any automated gate we build (e.g., the false-positive/isolation eligibility gate, EVL-10) — the gate's pass/fail must be recomputed from the recorded trial data, not read from an agent's self-assessment. Source: LESSONS 2026-09-25.
3. **"No anchors/matches found" must never silently mean "pass."** A verifier's lexical layers treated unrecognized entities/units/predicates as nothing to check, so made-up content passed; found only by testing with invented, non-gold inputs (new names colliding with rule vocabulary). Directly relevant to AST-7/EVL-5 (unsupported-flag detection) and SCN-5 (hidden role labels) — any checker in our pipeline must resolve-or-withhold, and must be tested against adversarially invented scenario entities/names, not only the gold fixture set. Source: LESSONS 2026-09-27/2026-10-04 (two entries, same lesson, found twice at different stages — itself evidence the lesson needs encoding as a standing test practice, not a one-off fix).
4. **Typed outputs over parsed prose, for anything a verifier must check.** Seven rounds of adversarial review each found a new way free-text judgment prose could be gamed (appositives moving an attribution, names containing reserved vocabulary words, negations). Fix: pipeline stages emit **typed objects** (subject id, predicate enum, confidence term, premise claim ids); display text is rendered FROM the typed object via fixed templates, so the verifier checks structure, not prose. This is highly relevant to AST-8's closed-category enum and to SCN-5/SCN-9's hidden scoring key — rendering findings from typed fields (rather than parsing free LLM prose for a "deceptive/truthful/insufficient" verdict) closes an entire adversarial-robustness class pre-emptively. Source: DECISIONS 2026-09-27 "Judgments are typed, rendered from templates."
5. **A relaxed test/gate expectation must name the exact permitted condition, or it silently hides real defects.** A smoke test was loosened to accept any "partial" result regardless of cause, so real data loss also passed; caught by a cold/adversarial review. Directly applicable to any of our own CI/gate work per the user's global "never rig CI" hard rule — any acceptable-non-pass state (e.g., "insufficient evidence" as a legitimate EVL/EX-3 outcome) must be scoped to its documented cause with a regression proving any OTHER cause of the same surface state still fails. Source: LESSONS 2026-09-27.
6. **Durable-execution/recovery semantics: a passing timing model can omit an unwritten recovery mechanism, and "failed response" ≠ "failed transaction."** Several entries (2026-09-25, recovery/ownership/lease bugs) show that reasoning about distributed retries/timeouts by instinct repeatedly produced wrong proofs; the fix pattern each time was to read the EXACT behavior of the pinned library version and test the stated numeric bound at its exact value plus one unit beyond. Relevant if our arena's scenario engine or run ledger uses any durable-execution/retry framework (SCN-10's "append-only trial event log," BKO's trial state machine): test boundary values exactly, and never infer a transaction's outcome from a client-side timeout alone.
7. **Equal input exposure is not equal treatment in an aggregate/leaderboard calculation.** A modulo-based rotation gave some entities permanently correlated exposure, skewing an award-share calculation even with balanced marginal counts. **Directly relevant to our BKO-1/BKO-7 "cross-pair assistant and subject candidates under equal budgets"** and the 3×3 bake-off matrix: any round-robin/rotation scheme for pairing assistants with subjects must be checked for joint-assignment bias (some assistant-subject pairs correlated more than others), not just marginal-count balance, with an adversarial permutation test before trusting the leaderboard. Source: LESSONS 2026-09-25 "Equal family exposure is not equal treatment."
8. **Catch-all/degenerate metadata collapses bidder/aggregate diversity; test on odd real-world inputs, not just clean fixtures.** A catch-all industry code dropped real rivals from consideration, and a flawed aggregation gave every non-winner a constant floor value. Analogous risk for our scenario engine: case families with edge-case metadata (SCN-2's "matched levels of difficulty," SCN-11's factorial style/difficulty/role design) must be tested on degenerate/odd generated cases, not only clean hand-authored ones, or the case-family balance (dev vs. hidden split, SCN-12) could silently collapse. Source: LESSONS 2026-10-04.
